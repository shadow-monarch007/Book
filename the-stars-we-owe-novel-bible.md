## CRITICAL RULES — NON-NEGOTIABLE

These rules apply to every single chapter, every scene, every line of dialogue. Violating them produces a book that feels like AI wrote it. Follow them and it feels like a person lived it.

1. **Never summarise emotion. Show the body.** Not "she felt nervous" — "she turned her coffee cup in both hands and didn't drink it." Not "he was angry" — "he set the glass down too carefully."
2. **Dialogue is incomplete. People don't finish thoughts.** They trail off. They change subject. They answer a different question than the one asked. They say fine when they mean the opposite.
3. **Every chapter ends on an image or an action. Never a feeling stated.** The last line of every chapter is physical, specific, and slightly open.
4. **Nex never announces himself.** He does not monologue. He does not explain his feelings. He acts on them badly, partially, in his own language. The reader understands him before Sophie does.
5. **Sophie is not a damsel. She is not rescued.** Her arc is about returning to herself — not being saved by a man. Nex accelerates her return but does not cause it.
6. **Time passes continuously.** Each arc is a continuation of the last. Characters reference previous events naturally. Friendships deepen. Habits form. People change gradually. Nothing resets.
7. **The steamy scenes are emotionally loaded.** They are not interludes. They happen because something broke or something built to breaking point. The emotion of the scene is inseparable from the physical content.
8. **Secondary characters have their own lives.** Jade has opinions and problems that have nothing to do with Sophie. Eli has a life outside Nex. Ryan is a person, not a plot device.
9. **No purple prose.** No "his smouldering gaze" or "her heart hammered wildly." Specific and earned always beats generic and decorative.
10. **Read the previous chapter before writing the next one.** Continuity is everything. Small details — a mug left on a windowsill, a text unanswered, a jacket left at someone's place — carry across chapters.

## PROSE STYLE — EMILY HENRY REGISTER

The target prose style is Emily Henry at her best — *People We Meet on Vacation*, *Happy Place*. This means:

**Sentence rhythm:** Mix of short punchy sentences and longer flowing ones. Never too many of the same length in a row. Rhythm creates emotion.

**Interiority:** First person OR close third person — choose one and never switch. The protagonist's thoughts are present but not overwhelming. We're in her head but she's also watching the world.

**Wit:** Sophie has a dry, warm wit. Nex has an even drier one. Their banter should make the reader smile before it makes them ache.

**Specificity:** "A coffee that tasted like it had been sitting since Tuesday" not "a bad coffee." Always the specific detail.

**Dialogue tags:** Mostly said and asked. Occasionally nothing — just action beats. Never "he smirked" or "she breathed."

**Example of the target voice:**

> The thing about Nex Maverick was that he made you feel like you were interrupting something important just by existing in his eyeline. He had that particular quality of stillness that some people mistake for depth and others mistake for rudeness. Sophie had made her decision on day one and stuck to it, the way you stick to a bad haircut — not because it was right but because admitting it was wrong felt worse.

## CHARACTERS — FULL PSYCHOLOGY

---

### NEX MAVERICK

**Age at story start:** 18 (first year)
**Appearance:** Tall, dark hair that's slightly too long, always looks like he forgot to care about how he looks but somehow it works. Sharp jaw. Eyes that stay on things longer than is comfortable. Usually in dark colours — not intentionally, it's just what he reaches for. Has a small scar above his left eyebrow from something he never explains.

**Surface presentation:** Cold. Nonchalant. The person everyone has a theory about. Doesn't perform warmth, doesn't perform interest. Responds to most social situations with the minimum viable engagement. People read this as arrogance. It is not arrogance.

**Interior reality:** Watches everything. Remembers everything. Feels things at a volume he has learned to keep turned down because the one time he turned it up the room emptied and he was left holding the speaker alone. Has an exact, quiet sense of humour that only surfaces when he forgets to guard it.

**The wound:** Age sixteen. A friend group — six people, the centre of his world for two years. He was the one who organised, who remembered birthdays, who showed up. One summer they simply moved on. Got new people, new things. Stopped calling. He realised months later that he had been the only one treating it as something permanent. Not betrayal — just the quiet discovery that he had loved something more than it loved him back. He has been waiting for that confirmation in every relationship since. It always comes. Until Eli. Until Sophie.

**Trust issues in practice:** He doesn't pull away dramatically. He just doesn't fully arrive. He keeps one foot out of every room. He waits for people to show him the exit before they find it themselves. He mistakes this for self-protection. It is also self-sabotage.

**What he's like when the wall drops:** Warmer than anyone expected. Quietly funny. Attentive in a way that feels like being actually seen — he notices the things people don't announce. He remembers what you said three weeks ago that you'd forgotten you said. When he decides someone is his, his loyalty is total and unperformed.

**His arc:** Learning that love is not a debt that gets called in. That some people stay not because they haven't found the exit yet but because they don't want it. Arriving fully. Turning the volume up.

**His voice in dialogue:** Dry. Economical. Occasionally devastating. He says a lot with very few words. Sometimes he says nothing and it says more.

**What he's like with Sophie specifically:** He has been noticing her since the orientation session. He does not examine why for a long time. Around her he is slightly less sealed than usual — not visibly, not in a way she'd notice early, but the reader sees it. He is more present. More careful. When she laughs at something he said he goes very still for a second.

---

### SOPHIE MCKENZIE

**Age at story start:** 19 (second year)
**Appearance:** Medium height, warm brown skin, natural hair she wears different ways — sometimes big and free, sometimes pulled up, the change usually reflects her mood. Bright eyes. The kind of face that makes strangers feel like they've met before. Wears colour. Wears things that have a small story — a bracelet from a market, earrings her mum gave her.

**Surface presentation:** Warm, social, the person who makes a group feel like a group. Remembers names. Asks follow-up questions. Genuinely interested in people. Everyone loves her.

**Interior reality:** The most misunderstood person in every room. People love the version of Sophie she performs — the warmth, the energy, the generosity. Very few people love the version that is tired, or uncertain, or quietly devastated that she has given everything to person after person and received careful holding back in return. She is slowly learning to give less so it hurts less when she gets less back. She does not want to be learning this.

**The wound:** Not one event. An accumulation. Every person she has loved fully has held her lightly. Her previous relationships. Ryan. The specific grief of a person who loves generously and keeps finding herself alone in that generosity.

**Her arc:** Not finding someone who finally deserves her. Finding herself — the lively, open, generous, loudly alive Sophie who existed before she learned that softness was something people took without reciprocating. Becoming that person again, with the depth of what she's been through underneath. And then, from that place of being herself again, choosing Nex.

**Her voice in dialogue:** Warm, slightly sharp, quick. She is funnier than people expect. When she's comfortable she has a particular kind of teasing wit that surfaces — playful but never cruel. When she's performing fineness her sentences get shorter and more careful.

**What she's like at home:** Completely different register. Looser. Louder. The Sophie from before. Her family gets this version automatically. When Nex sees it he is not prepared.

---

### ELI RHODES

**Age at story start:** 18 (first year, Nex's year)
**Appearance:** Average height, the kind of face you trust immediately. Usually wearing something slightly too casual for the occasion. Easy smile.

**Personality:** Calm where Nex is sealed. The kind of person who says the right thing not because he's wise but because he actually listens. Has never once made Nex explain himself — he just knows. His loyalty is not performed or announced. It's just consistently there, in small daily ways, without comment.

**His function in the story:** The moral compass who never preaches. The person who shows Nex what it looks like to be loved without it being taken away. The relationship between Nex and Eli is the novel's proof of concept — that Nex is capable of being close to someone, that the wall is a choice not a permanent condition.

**His arc:** Quietly steady throughout. His one important scene — when Nex finally says what Eli means to him, and Eli doesn't make it weird, doesn't leave, just says something simple and continues — is one of the novel's most important emotional moments.

**His voice:** Easy, warm, occasionally dry. He and Nex have a shorthand built from two years of proximity and trust. Their dialogue is often very short — the kind of conversation that only works because of the context behind it.

---

### JADE PARK

**Age at story start:** 19 (second year, Sophie's year and best friend)
**Appearance:** Sharp, put-together, the kind of person who always looks like they meant to look that way.

**Personality:** Protective of Sophie without being controlling. Sees Ryan clearly from early. Sees Nex more clearly than Sophie does, and earlier. Knows when to push and when to hold back — usually holds back longer than Sophie would want, acts when she can't anymore.

**Her arc:** She has her own life. Her own complicated thing with someone — a relationship of her own that runs parallel to Sophie's story, surfaces occasionally, never takes over. She is not a supporting character who exists only to react to Sophie's choices. She has opinions, bad days, her own growth.

**Her voice:** Sharp, warm, the friend who will tell you the truth wrapped in enough love that you can hear it.

---

### RYAN CALLOWAY

**Age at story start:** 20
**Appearance:** Good-looking in a conventional way. Easy to like in a group.

**Personality:** Charming socially, distracted intimately. Not malicious. Holds Sophie lightly without examining whether that's fair. The tragedy of Ryan is that he's not bad enough to leave cleanly — he's just not enough, and Sophie has to arrive at that understanding slowly.

**His exit:** Not a dramatic betrayal. A quiet accumulation of being someone's second thought. When it ends the reader should feel the specific grief of that — the loss not of a bad person but of the person you thought he was going to become.

---

### NEX'S PARENTS — DANIEL AND MARIE MAVERICK

**Daniel:** Warm, slightly embarrassing in a dad way, makes terrible puns and is delighted by them. The kind of father who claps Nex on the shoulder and says something that sounds simple but lands deep. Has a workshop in the garage he's always in the middle of some project in.

**Marie:** Laughs easily, feeds people, has an opinion about everything and shares it without apology. Loved by everyone immediately. Completely different in register from how Nex presents — which is the point. He came from warmth. The coldness was learned outside the home.

**When Sophie meets them:** She is not prepared for how warm they are. She keeps looking at Nex as if checking whether he came from these people. He watches her figure it out with something close to vulnerability — this is the version of him that existed before the world taught him to seal.

---

### SOPHIE'S FAMILY

**Her mum — Patricia McKenzie:** Sees through Sophie's performed fineness immediately and always. Doesn't push — just makes space. One quiet conversation between Patricia and Sophie is one of the novel's most important scenes.

**Her younger sibling — Marcus, 16:** Adores Sophie in the particular way of a younger sibling who would never say so. Teases her constantly. Is immediately suspicious of Nex and then grudgingly won over.

**The hometown:** Feels like the version of Sophie that existed before university. Warmer, louder, more chaotic. The house has photos everywhere. Something is always cooking. Nex arrives and stands in the middle of it slightly overwhelmed and Sophie watches him look at everything and feels something she doesn't examine yet.

## ARC ONE — ARRIVALS
*September–December, University Year One*
*Chapters 1–7*
*POV: Alternating Sophie and Nex — establish both voices early*

**The emotional job of this arc:** Establish the world, the characters, the central tension. Plant the first seeds. Make the reader care about Sophie and Nex independently before the story puts them together.

**Chapter 1 — Sophie**
Sophie's morning. Second year energy — she knows this campus, loves it, belongs here. Ryan texted at 1am and didn't explain why. She's not worried. She loves him. The welcome session — she runs it because she loves this, being the person who makes newcomers feel like they belong. A first year at the back who clearly doesn't want to be here. She tries to draw him in. He responds with something flat and dry. She writes him off. He watches her run the rest of the session without saying anything else.
*End image: Sophie walking home with Jade, telling the story of the rude first year. Making it funny. Not thinking about it again.*

**Chapter 2 — Nex**
The same day from Nex's POV. His first week. The housing coordinator dragging him to orientation. Eli already his anchor — they met in the first two days, the friendship formed fast and without ceremony the way Nex's real connections always do. The campus through his eyes — watchful, quieter, more observant than Sophie's version. What he notices about the girl running the session. He does not name what he notices. He stays until the end.
*End image: Nex and Eli walking back to halls. Eli asks what he thought. Nex says "it was fine." Beat. "She runs a tight session."  Eli looks at him. Nex doesn't look back.*

**Chapter 3 — Sophie**
First proper encounter outside orientation — a shared class, a library, a campus space. The friction. Sophie's irritation at his presence. He says something that reads as dismissive. She responds with her particular brand of warm sharpness. He almost smiles. She doesn't see it.
*End image: Sophie dropping her coffee card on the way out. Finding it on her desk the next morning. No note.*

**Chapter 4 — Sophie**
Sophie and Ryan. A proper evening together — dinner, his room, the relationship in full colour. Her warmth, his distraction, the way she fills the silences he leaves with cheerful noise. She loves him. That's real. But the reader sees, in the specific details of how he holds his phone at dinner, what this actually is.
*End image: Sophie walking back to her room at midnight, texting Jade "good night?" Jade replies "he good?" Sophie types "yeah, fine." Sends it. Looks at it.*

**Chapter 5 — Nex**
Eli and Nex. Their dynamic in full — the shorthand, the loyalty that lives in small daily actions. Eli asks about someone from Nex's past — not Sophie, someone before, one of the friend group. Nex's response tells us everything about the wound without naming it directly. Eli doesn't push. Just stays.
*End image: Eli falling asleep mid-conversation. Nex finishing the episode they were watching alone. Turning it off quietly. Not leaving.*

**Chapter 6 — Sophie**
The first crack in the Sophie/Ryan armour — something small he forgets or prioritises away from. Sophie excuses it. Later, on her way back from the library, she passes Nex sitting outside with a younger student — a fresher who looks lost. Nex is talking to him in a low, unhurried way. Not performing kindness. Just — there. Sophie stops. Watches for a second. Files it away incorrectly — tells herself it was probably just someone he knew.
*End image: Sophie in bed that night, opening her notes app to add something, finding an old list she made of things she was excited about this year. Reading it. Closing it without adding anything.*

**Chapter 7 — Nex**
Something happens that puts Nex and Sophie in the same space for an extended time — a group dinner, a department event, something neither chose but both attend. Their first real conversation. Not warm — but not the performed flatness either. Something underneath the surface makes itself briefly visible. She asks him something direct. He answers honestly, slightly more honestly than he meant to. She looks at him differently for a moment. Then Jade calls her over and the moment closes.
*End image: Nex walking home. Eli texting "how was it?" Nex: "fine." Eli: "yeah?" Nex puts his phone in his pocket without replying.*

## ARC TWO — THE FESTIVAL
*February–March, University Year One*
*Chapters 8–15*
*POV: Mostly Sophie with two Nex chapters*

**The emotional job of this arc:** The first time Nex sees Sophie fully — in her element, free, not managing anyone. The reader watches him watch her. The first almost-honest moment between them. Ryan's carelessness starts becoming visible even to Sophie.

**Chapter 8 — Sophie**
Festival preparation week. Sophie in her element — organising, delegating, laughing, completely alive. Jade beside her. Ryan promises to come to the opening night and Sophie doesn't tell Jade how much she's been looking forward to it.
*End image: Opening night. Ryan texts at 7:43pm: "might be late, don't wait for me." Sophie puts her phone face-down. Goes out to the crowd. Smiles.*

**Chapter 9 — Nex**
Eli drags Nex to a festival event — something neither of them would have chosen. The campus transformed. Nex watching it all with his usual slight remove. And then Sophie — performing, presenting, running something on the main stage — and the remove disappears. He watches. Doesn't leave when he could have left twenty minutes ago. Eli notices. Says nothing.
*End image: Walking back. Eli: "good event." Nex: "yeah." Long pause. "She's good at that." Eli looks at him. "At what?" Nex doesn't answer.*

**Chapter 10 — Sophie**
Ryan makes it to one festival event — the wrong one, the smaller one, the one Sophie wasn't invested in. She is grateful anyway. They have a good night. But there's a moment where she's telling him something she's proud of from the week and she watches him look over her shoulder at someone across the room. She finishes the sentence. He says "that's great, babe." She smiles. Something in her goes a little quieter.
*End image: Sophie lying in bed that night. Ryan asleep. Making a list in her notes app of things she hasn't told him because they wouldn't land.*

**Chapter 11 — Sophie**
Festival midpoint. Sophie, Jade, a group of their friends, an evening event with food and music. Nex is there — Eli again, probably. Sophie and Nex end up near each other at the food stalls. The conversation this time is different — easier, slightly warmer, the friction lower. He says something dry and she laughs before she can decide not to. She doesn't like how natural that felt.
*End image: Jade watching Sophie walk back toward the group. Saying nothing. Eating her food.*

**Chapter 12 — Sophie**
The jealousy seed — someone in their wider group is clearly interested in Sophie. Pays her sustained attention at a festival event. Sophie is flattered and slightly uncomfortable. Nex is across the room. She happens to look over. He is watching. Not with an obvious expression — just watching. Their eyes meet for a second. He looks away first. She turns back to the conversation. Her pulse is doing something she ignores.
*End image: Walking home later. Sophie, Jade, and a few others. Sophie keeps almost asking Jade something. Doesn't.*

**Chapter 13 — Nex**
Nex's perspective on the festival jealousy scene. What he actually felt. How he handled it — or didn't. A conversation with Eli that gets closer to honesty than Nex usually allows before he closes it off. The moment he acknowledges to himself, without language, that something is different about this. He doesn't follow that thought. He turns it off.
*End image: Nex on his phone. Sophie's name not in his contacts yet. He doesn't examine why he noticed that.*

**Chapter 14 — Sophie**
The final night of the festival. Ryan doesn't come. Sophie knew he wouldn't, had been unconsciously preparing, which is its own kind of heartbreak. She is fine. She is brilliant at being fine. Late, most people gone, lanterns or string lights still up. Nex is there — end of the night, crossing paths while she's packing down a stall. They talk for twenty minutes. Real talk. Not the performed versions of themselves. Something honest gets exchanged without either of them naming it.
*End image: Sophie walking home alone under the lights still up. Texting Ryan "good night." Getting a thumbs up emoji. Looking at it for a second. Putting her phone away.*

**Chapter 15 — Sophie**
The morning after the festival ends. Campus back to normal. Sophie sits with what the week was — the good parts, the Ryan-shaped gap in the middle of them. Jade says something gentle about Ryan. Sophie deflects. But less smoothly than usual. 
*End image: Sophie opens the notes app. Starts a new list. Titles it, then deletes the title. Closes the app.*

## ARC THREE — THE SUMMER
*May–August, Between Years One and Two*
*Chapters 16–20*
*POV: Alternating — the separation felt in both directions*

**The emotional job of this arc:** Time passes. The shift that happened during the festival lives in the gap. Both of them feeling the absence of the other without naming it. Sophie and Ryan's relationship entering a new, quieter, harder phase. Nex and Eli's friendship deepening.

**Chapter 16 — Sophie**
End of year. The dispersal. Sophie going home. The particular mood of a campus emptying — things left undone, conversations that didn't happen. She runs into Nex on the last day — not planned. Brief. He says something small that she will think about three times over the summer without meaning to.
*End image: Sophie on the train home. Watching the city become countryside. Playing a song that was playing during the festival.*

**Chapter 17 — Nex**
Nex's summer. He stays near campus or goes somewhere quiet. Eli is there. A long-form scene of them just existing together in the summer — some day trip, a project, something ordinary. And underneath it, the thing Nex hasn't said to Eli, the thank you he can't find the language for. He almost says it once. Doesn't.
*End image: Nex and Eli watching something bad on TV at midnight. Eli falls asleep again. Nex looks at him. Looks away.*

**Chapter 18 — Sophie**
Sophie at home. Her family — warm, chaotic, real. Patricia seeing through Sophie's performed fineness within the first day. Marcus teasing her. Sophie relaxing into the version of herself that exists here. Ryan visits for a week. The family is polite. Marcus is quiet in a way he isn't with Sophie's friends. Patricia says nothing specific but Sophie catches her watching Ryan once with a careful expression.
*End image: Ryan leaving. Sophie waving from the door. Her mum standing beside her. Neither of them says anything.*

**Chapter 19 — Sophie**
Mid-summer. Sophie rebuilds herself slightly — a job she likes, time with old friends from home, a creative project or something she's been putting off. The lively girl surfacing in flashes. One evening she's laughing at something — really laughing, unguarded — and catches herself and realises she hasn't done that in a while. Thinks about who she has been lately. Doesn't like the answer.
*End image: Sophie starting a new notes list. This one she keeps.*

**Chapter 20 — Nex**
Late summer. Something from Nex's past resurfaces — a message from someone from the old friend group. Not malicious, just — contact, out of nowhere, as if no time passed. Nex's response to it. The way old wounds announce themselves. Eli watching him process it. And then, unprompted, Nex says something about going back to campus that is clearly about more than campus. Eli understands. Doesn't push.
*End image: Nex packing his bag for second year. Finding something from first year at the bottom. Pausing. Putting it in anyway.*

## ARC FOUR — THE SHIFT
*September–December, University Year Two*
*Chapters 21–28*
*POV: Mostly Sophie, two Nex chapters*

**The emotional job of this arc:** The proximity intensifies. Chemistry building through sustained contact. Sophie's theory of Nex fully dismantled. The first almost-kiss. Ryan's carelessness becoming undeniable.

**Chapter 21 — Sophie**
Back on campus. Second year, Nex now a second year too. The dynamic different — they have history now, even if neither of them would call it that. The first time they see each other this year. How it feels different. She doesn't want it to feel different.
*End image: Sophie telling Jade about running into him. Jade: "and?" Sophie: "nothing, it was normal." Jade eating her lunch. "Okay."*

**Chapter 22 — Sophie**
The shared project — a class assignment, a department initiative, something that forces them to work together regularly. First proper work session. The friction lower than expected. His working style revealed: thorough, slightly perfectionist, dry commentary on everything. She finds it more entertaining than she wants to.
*End image: First work session ending. Sophie walking out. Realising she forgot her jacket. Going back. He's already holding it out.*

**Chapter 23 — Nex**
Nex's experience of the shared project. What he notices about how she works. The moment he realises the wall he usually maintains around her is not maintaining itself the way it should. His internal management of this. Eli asking how the project is going. Nex's answer being slightly too measured.
*End image: Nex at his desk late. The project document open. A message from Sophie about something small — a question about formatting. He answers. She sends back a single line that makes him exhale through his nose. He doesn't examine what that was.*

**Chapter 24 — Sophie**
Sophie catches Nex being tender with someone — a younger student struggling with something, Eli after something difficult. The version of him she was never supposed to see. Her entire theory of who he is collapses. She is annoyed about this. She had a perfectly functional understanding of him and he has quietly dismantled it.
*End image: Sophie telling Jade what she saw. Jade: "surprised?" Sophie: "no." Beat. "Yes."*

**Chapter 25 — Sophie**
A night out — group of friends, a bar, easy atmosphere. Sophie and Nex both there. Drinks loosening things. The first time they're genuinely playful with each other — the sharp banter with warmth underneath it, the specific register of two people who have been circling each other and accidentally landed in the same orbit. At some point they're in a corner talking and the rest of the group has faded out. She notices this. He notices her notice.
*End image: Sophie and Jade walking home. Jade says "you were talking to him for like an hour." Sophie: "was I?" Jade looks at her. Sophie looks at the pavement.*

**Chapter 26 — Sophie**
The study session that runs too late. Just the two of them, project deadline, the library or one of their rooms. Tiredness dropping defences. A conversation that goes somewhere neither expected — something real, slightly personal, the first genuine window into who he actually is. He tells her something small about himself that he doesn't usually tell people. She tells him something back. Neither of them comments on what just happened.
*End image: Sophie walking home at 2am. The campus quiet. She keeps replaying one specific thing he said.*

**Chapter 27 — Sophie**
The almost-kiss. Specific, physical, interrupted. They are close — too close, the moment has been building and both of them know it and neither has moved. Something is about to happen. Interrupted by something external — a friend arriving, a phone call, a door. The moment closes. Both of them step back. Neither addresses it. Sophie goes home and lies in her dark room staring at the ceiling.
*End image: Sophie's phone lights up. Ryan: "you up?" She stares at it. Types "yeah." Sends it. Puts the phone face-down.*

**Chapter 28 — Sophie**
Ryan. A scene where his carelessness is finally too large to excuse smoothly. Not a betrayal — just a moment of being unmistakably, specifically someone's second thought. Sophie holds herself together. She is an expert at holding herself together. But something shifts. The reader feels it even if Sophie won't name it yet.
*End image: Sophie on the phone with her mum that night. Patricia asking how she is. Sophie saying "fine, yeah, everything's fine." A pause. Patricia: "okay, love." Sophie: "mum—" She stops. "Nothing. I'll call tomorrow."*

## ARC FIVE — THE TRIP
*January–February, University Year Two Semester Two*
*Chapters 29–37*
*POV: Alternating — this arc needs both perspectives*

**The emotional job of this arc:** Maximum tension. Forced proximity. The almost that actually almost happens. The jealousy scene. The electric night. Everything the reader has been waiting for, served slowly and then all at once. And then morning, and the retreat.

**Chapter 29 — Sophie**
The trip announced — a group of eight or so, a rented house somewhere. Beach, mountains, city, your choice but somewhere that requires a long drive and close quarters for several days. Sophie almost doesn't go. Jade tells her she's going.
*End image: Sophie packing. Putting in one more thing she doesn't need. Then taking it out. Then putting it back.*

**Chapter 30 — Nex**
Nex's decision to go. Eli is going. Simple. The night before — Nex quieter than usual, Eli not asking why.
*End image: Nex checking his bag. Phone face-down on the bed. One message unread. He leaves it.*

**Chapter 31 — Sophie**
The drive. Long, confined, music, the specific intimacy of a car journey. Sophie and Nex end up in the same row or front and back. The conversation that only happens in moving vehicles — slightly more honest than the stationary versions. Something is said that neither of them can un-say.
*End image: Arriving. The house. Everyone spilling out. Sophie and Nex the last ones out of the car. A second where they're just standing there before the group absorbs them.*

**Chapter 32 — Sophie**
First evening — group dynamic, drinks, the house becoming a world. Sophie relaxing into it. Nex on the edge of the group but present. A moment at the kitchen counter — just them, briefly, getting drinks, the house noise behind them. Easy. Too easy. Sophie notices.
*End image: Late night, most people gone to bed. Sophie sitting outside. Nex coming out for a reason that's probably not the reason. They sit together. The chapter ends on them sitting, the dark, the quiet.*

**Chapter 33 — Nex**
The jealousy scene. Someone in the group — call him Marco, easy with his attention, clearly interested in Sophie — spends the day being attentive to her. Sophie, who has been closed lately, responds to the attention the way a plant responds to light. Nex watches this from various distances throughout the day. His response is controlled. Specific. Unmistakable only to the reader and, eventually, to Sophie. One moment where his jaw tightens and his eyes go flat and he says something that cuts Marco off without being rude. Sophie sees it. Can't unfeel it.
*End image: Nex going to bed early. Lying in the dark. The specific physical discomfort of feeling something he told himself he wasn't feeling.*

**Chapter 34 — Sophie**
The jealousy scene from Sophie's side. What she felt watching it. The conversation with Jade in the shared room: "did you see—" / "yes" / "he wasn't—" / "Sophie." / "I'm not saying anything." / "I know." The thing she can't un-know now.
*End image: Sophie staring at the ceiling. The house quiet. A door down the hall.*

**Chapter 35 — Sophie**
The almost-kiss that actually almost happens. The specific scene — late, most people asleep, a moment of aloneness that neither of them engineered but neither left. Close enough. The tension physical and undeniable. His eyes dropping to her mouth. Her not moving away. A breath of space between them. Something almost crosses. Interrupted — barely, just enough. They separate. Neither speaks. Sophie goes to bed.
*End image: Sophie lying in the dark. Her heart. The ceiling. The sound of the house.*

**Chapter 36 — Nex**
The same almost from his side. What it took not to close that last inch. Why he didn't. The trust issue surfacing at exactly the wrong moment — the voice that says if you do this she will become someone who left. He goes to bed. Does not sleep.
*End image: 4am. Nex at the window. The outside dark and quiet. His hand on the glass.*

**Chapter 37 — Sophie**
The electric night — the next night, inhibitions lower, less managed, something nearly crosses a different line. Not the kiss — further. In the dark, the house asleep, two people who have been circling each other for eighteen months. It nearly happens. In some versions it does happen — first intimate scene, charged, desperate, the emotional intensity of too long waited. Morning arrives. Both of them reset. The drive home. Everything different. Nothing said. The reader devastated.

*If they sleep together:* The scene is visceral, emotionally loaded, specific. Not romanticised — real and urgent and suffused with eighteen months of waiting. In the morning the tone shifts. Neither of them knows how to be in the daylight version of what just happened.

*End image: The drive home. Sophie in the passenger seat. The landscape going past. Nex driving. Neither of them has turned the music on.*

## ARC SIX — THE BREAKING
*March–June, University Year Two End*
*Chapters 38–44*
*POV: Sophie primarily, one Nex chapter*

**The emotional job of this arc:** Everything breaks. Ryan ends — quietly, devastatingly. Sophie closes. Nex reaches badly. His trust issues and her closed walls collide. The reader's lowest point.

**Chapter 38 — Sophie**
The Ryan ending. Not one moment — the accumulation finally heavy enough. Sophie realising she has been maintaining the relationship alone for longer than she can count. The specific grief of ending something that was real, that she loved, that simply was never what she needed it to be.

**Chapter 39 — Sophie**
The aftermath. Sophie constructing fineness carefully. Her friends around her. Jade holding the space without filling it. The lively girl going quieter, more internal. She is polite and present and fine. She is not fine.

**Chapter 40 — Sophie**

She should understand. She is too closed right now to understand it as what it is. She sees it as pity, or obligation, or something she can't receive in her current state.
*End image: Sophie looking at the thing he did. Not knowing what to do with it. Closing the door gently.*

**Chapter 41 — Nex**
What Nex was trying to do. The specific nature of his reach — how much it cost him, how much of the wall he moved to do it. Her response. The familiar confirmation arriving: see, you reached, and here is the result. He uses this to justify retreating.

**Chapter 42 — Sophie**
The collision — they are in the same place and something gets said. Not a screaming fight — something worse, quieter, more final. The specific thing said that opens the distance. Both of their wounds speaking. Her: "I'm not doing this again." Him: "I'm not asking you to." The space opens.

The weeks after. Separate. Both present on campus, in peripheral orbit. The specific discomfort of someone being nearby and unreachable. Sophie noticing the absence in the specific texture of days — moments she almost says something, places she looks before she remembers.

**Chapter 44 — Sophie**
End of year two. The summer coming again. Sophie sitting with what happened — not resolved, not over, just — set aside, because the year is ending and she needs to breathe.

## STEAMY SCENE GUIDELINES

**Heat level:** *Ugly Love* register. Explicit, visceral, emotionally charged. The physical content is inseparable from the emotional content — every steamy scene is also an emotional scene.

**Scene One (Arc Five, Chapter 37):**
Desperate. Urgent. Eighteen months of tension finally breaking. The emotional note is: *we shouldn't and we are anyway and neither of us is going to stop.* Physical description is specific and real. Afterwards: the reset, the morning, the retreat. The steamy scene should hurt as much as it satisfies because of what comes after.

**Scene Two (Arc Twelve, Chapter 77):**
Slower. Deliberate. The emotional note is: *I am choosing you fully and you are choosing me back and we both know it this time.* This scene has the weight of everything that came before it. It should be more emotionally devastating than the first one, even if less frantic.

**Rules for both:**
- Stay in POV — the reader feels everything through Sophie or Nex, not from outside
- Physical detail specific and real, not romanticised or generic
- Emotion present throughout — not a break from the story, part of it
- Aftermath matters as much as the scene — how they are immediately after
- No purple prose — "smouldering" and "heaving" are banned

## CHAPTER WRITING INSTRUCTIONS FOR THE AGENT

**Before writing each chapter:**
1. Read the chapter brief above completely
2. Read the previous chapter's end image — your chapter begins in the same world
3. Identify which characters are present and what each of them wants in this scene
4. Identify the emotional job of this chapter — what must the reader feel by the end
5. Identify the end image — know where you're landing before you start

**Chapter structure:**
- Open in scene, not summary. First line in the moment, not describing it.
- Build through specific physical detail and dialogue
- At least one moment per chapter that will make the reader feel something — laugh, ache, grip the book
- End image: physical, specific, slightly open. Never a feeling stated.

**Length:** 1,800 to 2,500 words per chapter. Some may run longer for major scenes (trip arc, steamy scenes, confession). None shorter than 1,500.

**POV consistency:** Choose Sophie or Nex at the top of each chapter. Do not switch mid-chapter. First person or close third — choose at the start and never change.

**Voice check before submitting each chapter:**
- Does Nex announce his feelings anywhere? If yes, remove it.
- Does Sophie perform fineness and is it visible to the reader even when she believes it? If not, add it.
- Does the dialogue sound like people or like characters? Read it aloud.
- Does the end image land? Is it specific enough to be visual?

## WHAT THE AUDITOR (CLAUDE) WILL CHECK ON EACH ARC

When you return each completed arc for audit, the following will be assessed:

1. **Voice consistency** — Does Nex sound like Nex throughout? Does Sophie's interiority feel real?
2. **Continuity** — Are details from previous arcs carried forward correctly?
3. **Emotional pacing** — Is the tension building correctly? Are we moving toward the right things?
4. **The motifs** — Are the recurring objects and images being used correctly?
5. **Dialogue** — Does it sound like people?
6. **The end images** — Do they land?
7. **The steamy scenes** — Are they emotionally loaded or just physical?
8. **Overall feel** — Does this arc feel like a continuation of time, not a collection of scenes?

Any arc that fails more than two of these checks goes back for a rewrite pass before the next arc is written.
